2026 goals

Set January 2026 — the plan everything gets measured against.

Financial▾

Built on an assumed $2,500–$3,000 monthly income. The annual savings target is $10,000, with tithing fixed at $250 every month regardless of how the month goes. Monthly spending is capped at $2,500, and checking is never supposed to drop below $1,000.

Starting account balances, tracked as the baseline for the year: Roth IRA $10,000, traditional brokerage $35,000, Robinhood crypto $4,000, 403(b) $6,200. Every month reports current balances and notes the change relative to these starting points — the goal is transparency and a clear view of the net worth trajectory, not just the savings number.

Physical▾

The primary standard is the Army Fitness Test: hold a minimum of 90 points in every event, Male 22–26. That means a 230 lb deadlift for 3 reps, 43 hand-release push-ups, a 2:25 plank, and a 15:50 two-mile run. Sprint-drag-carry (2:58 standard) is explicitly excluded from direct training — no sled or 90 lb setup available outside Army-led testing, so it only gets trained during actual drill.

Underneath the AFT, training requires at least one full push/pull/legs cycle per week and one dedicated cardio session per week. Separately, personal gym PR goals for the year: deadlift 315×8, barbell bench press 225×4, cable triceps 80×8, dumbbell shoulder press 120×6, dumbbell curl 90×4, cable crunch 180×6.

Spiritual▾

Sermon notes for every Sunday attended, plus a weekly summary entry covering Bible reading and prayer — short, meant for continuity rather than a detailed report. The reading plan moves through one book every two months: Mark in January–February, Luke in March–April, Romans in May–June, 2 Corinthians in July–August, 1 Timothy in September–October, and 2 Timothy to close the year in November–December.

Intellectual▾

A new focus topic every month: January personal finance and economics, February STEM fundamentals, March U.S. or world history, April leadership and organizational behavior, May psychology, June military history or doctrine, July creativity and design, August philosophy or ethics, September law and civics, October health and human performance, November technology trends, and December is reserved for synthesis and review of the whole year’s learning.

The input requirement is 6–8 hours of audio per month plus 3–5 short articles from at least two different sources. Each month closes with one piece of output — a 500–700 word synthesis memo, a 10–12 bullet brief, or a 5-minute voice memo — and gets checked with a recall test (writing it out from memory, no notes) and a transfer test (applying it to work, finances, fitness, leadership, or a current event).

Creative▾

Two hard numbers for JD Meyers Productions this year: complete 3–4 weddings, and complete six photo shoots total, whether paid or unpaid.

Career thread▾

Not one of the five formally tracked pillars above — a narrative that runs underneath all of them. No single path was picked in January. The plan was to run every viable option at once (Air Force OTS, civil service, the airlines) and let the year itself narrow the field down, rather than betting everything on one door early.

A note on the physical numbers

The January plan set the AFT goal at a 90-point minimum per event — 230 lb deadlift, 43 push-ups, a 2:25 plank, 15:50 two-mile run. Later months’ dashboards tracked toward a visibly higher personal bar (350 lb, 61 reps, 3:35, 16:36) without ever renaming it as a change. That looks like a drift toward the AFT’s 100-point max scoring rather than a new decision, but it was never written down as one. This scorecard uses the actual January numbers.
