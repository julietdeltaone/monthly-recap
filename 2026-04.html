What the logs actually showThe ceiling speed isn't moving yet — 12mph both times — and that's fine. The increments are underneath it: 10 more seconds at peak, 1mph higher on the recovery set, staying at 5.0 between sets instead of jumping off to 3.5. These are exactly the right kind of small wins. The goal isn't a new top speed every session, it's extending how long you can live at 12 and raising the floor you come back to.
The consistent patternForm breaks down on the final set every time — not from speed, but from accumulated fatigue. Apr 6 the culprit was food timing (PB muffin) which tanked set 2's recovery and left nothing for set 3. Apr 21 the session was cleaner but set 3 still degraded. That third set is the adaptation frontier. The rest quality improvement (staying at 5.0 not 3.5) between sets is addressing it directly — more active recovery means a higher floor entering set 3.

On Shipping the Inventory Project

The inventory system started as a Notion database, became a flat HTML file, then became a two-file web app backed by Supabase — all in 3.5 weeks. The migration decision was right each time it was made. None of it was overengineered before the problem was actually understood. 528 items cataloged. The AI photo-parse workflow cut data entry time by roughly 60%.

The column visibility bug that kept recurring was a reminder that bugs at the intersection of state and rendering always hide in the same place: fix the state but forget to re-run the display logic. Once applyColPrefs() was wired to fire after every renderTable(), it stopped. The open items — PWA for locate.html, second bulk enrich pass, barn memorabilia totes — are documented and don't block use of the system.
