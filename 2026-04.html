Sermon 01 · April 5, 2026
Easter — "Why Do You Seek the Living Among the Dead?" — Luke 24 / Genesis 3 / Isaiah 53
Calvary Baptist Church · Guest Speaker: Ken Wilson
Luke 24Isaiah 53Passover / ResurrectionSatan's Attempts to Destroy

Overview

Easter message tracing the thread from Genesis 3 through Isaiah 53 to the empty tomb. The sermon built the case that Satan tried to destroy God's plan at every stage — the fall, the wilderness temptation, the cross — and failed at every one. The resurrection is the proof of that failure.

1. Satan Has One Playbook and It Doesn't Work

From Genesis 3 to the wilderness to the cross, the pattern is the same: target the most vulnerable moment, use deception or brute force, try to kill what God promised. At every point the plan failed. The attempt on the infant Jesus in Matthew 2. The wilderness temptation after 40 days of fasting. The cross itself, which Satan designed and which turned out to be the mechanism of his own defeat.

2. The Resurrection Was Prophesied Before Anyone Was Looking for It

Isaiah 53:10 describes the suffering servant dying and then prolonging his days — a resurrection embedded in a prophecy that predates Christ by centuries. The Passover lamb's blood on the doorposts foreshadows Christ's blood as the covering for death. These weren't retrofitted — they were woven in from the beginning.

3. The Question at the Tomb Applies Now

The angels' question to the women — "Why do you seek the living among the dead?" — applies to anyone looking for life in anything other than Christ. The tomb being empty is the only claim in history that changed the world because it was verifiable and was never successfully refuted.

Application

Satan has no peace to offer — that's his only blind spot. Peace is one thing he cannot counterfeit. Offering to pray for someone's peace is one of the most effective witnessing tools available because nobody turns it down.

The detail about Satan having no counterfeit for peace stuck with me. Every other spiritual-sounding thing — wisdom, power, fulfillment, community — he can fake well enough to fool people for a while. Peace, actual peace, he can't replicate. That's a practical evangelism insight, not just a theological one. The person who has nothing to lose by trying the prayer is the exact person who needs it most.
Sermon 02 · April 19, 2026
Acts 10 — Cornelius, Peter, and the Limits of Religion
Calvary Baptist Church · Pastor Brad
Acts 10CorneliusPeter's VisionHoly Spirit on Gentiles

Overview

Acts 10 centers on two men — a Roman centurion and a Jewish apostle — who both needed their assumptions corrected. Cornelius was a genuinely good man, devout, generous, prayerful, and still unsaved. Peter was committed to the gospel and still operating with ethnic walls God was about to tear down.

1. Being a Good Person Is Not Enough

Cornelius checks every box a man could check in religious sincerity: feared God, prayed always, gave generously, ran a household that followed his lead. The Bible even calls him "devout." And yet the angel sends him to find Peter — because he still needed to hear the gospel and receive the Holy Spirit. Good religious behavior with no actual encounter with Christ is where most of the American church lives.

2. God Shows No Partiality — and Doesn't Ask Our Permission

The sheet vision given to Peter was as much for him as for Cornelius. Peter would not have walked into a Gentile's home without divine instruction — it violated everything in his religious framework. God's plan for the gospel required dismantling the ethnic and religious walls Peter had built. He obeyed, preached, and the Holy Spirit fell on the Gentiles mid-sentence. God didn't wait for Peter to finish. He didn't ask whether the theological framework was ready.

Application

Your relationship with God through Jesus Christ does not depend on any church, any ceremony, any tradition, or any priest. Christ died to give direct access. The walls that religion builds around God are exactly what the gospel is designed to tear down.

The Cornelius picture is the uncomfortable one. He's not a skeptic, not a rebel, not someone you'd look at and say "he needs Jesus." He's doing everything. The gap between religious sincerity and actual salvation is narrow enough that a lot of people never realize they're on the wrong side of it. What makes the difference, based on this chapter, is the Holy Spirit's actual presence — not behavior, not effort, not church attendance. That's either deeply reassuring or deeply unsettling depending on where you're standing.
Sermon 03 · April 26, 2026
Romans 11 — Grace, the Remnant, and God's Unsearchable Plan
Calvary Baptist Church · Pastor Brad
Romans 11Grace vs. WorksJewish RemnantIsrael

Overview

Romans 11 addresses what looks like God abandoning Israel and concludes the opposite — that God's hardening of a portion of Israel was intentional, purposeful, and will result in their eventual reconciliation. The chapter is also the clearest definition of grace in the New Testament.

1. Grace Is Not Just Forgiveness — It's Forgiveness With a Plan

Mercy means you deserve punishment and aren't getting it. Grace goes further: you deserve punishment, you're not getting it, and God has a specific plan for your life. The distinction matters because grace is not passive. It's active. God's plan for the 7,000 who hadn't bowed to Baal wasn't just to spare them — it was to use them.

2. God's Sovereignty Doesn't Look Fair — and That's the Point

God purposely blinded a portion of Israel so that the gospel would go to the Gentiles, who would then make Israel jealous, which would eventually bring Israel back. The whole architecture is counterintuitive. Paul's response isn't to explain it — it's to declare that God's ways are past finding out and that the appropriate response is humility and prayer, not analysis.

Application

When God's plan doesn't make sense — and especially when it looks unfair — the prescribed posture is humility, not argument. Pray for the peace of Jerusalem. Pray for those who are currently blinded. Resist the pull of the political vortex that wants to make Israel vs. Palestine into a left/right issue. It isn't. The real division is always Christ or not Christ.

The "vortex" illustration landed. Washington is evil — not one side of it, all of it. Christians getting pulled into partisan camps, thinking alignment with "good morality" on one side makes them spiritually safe, is the exact trap Paul is describing. The real division has always been Christ vs. not-Christ, and anything that blurs that line — even things with good optics — is working against the gospel.

Career Situation — Where Things Actually Stand

Three paths are in motion. United Airlines is the most likely near-term outcome if anything lands — internal referral, advanced past resume screen, genuine willingness to relocate. The honest probability is 35%, which sounds low until you consider the other two.

OTS is the right long-term military play. The structural argument is strong — the contract is fulfilled regardless, the DD-368 is conditional by definition, and commissioning to active duty is the kind of move a unit can respect even if they'd rather keep you. SSG Wilson's read was that it has real possibility. But "real possibility" and "approved" are different things, and the state AG is the final authority. 20%.

Civil service is the third attempt at the same wall. The honest read on the prior failures points to a profile mismatch, not a competence gap — the personality-weighted disposition questions favor rule-bound, authority-deferring profiles, and that's not the profile that builds a multimedia business, pursues an officer commission, and thinks across multiple career paths simultaneously. That doesn't mean don't try. The exam costs nothing. Local law enforcement — particularly Deputy Sheriff — would be compatible with Guard obligations and has the lowest life disruption of any of the three. 15%.

What the situation has going for it: none of these paths are being pursued at the expense of the others. The MBA, the clearance, the Guard service, the business — they're not random. They're a deliberate accumulation of options. The July–August crunch window (OTS deadline July 4, AFOQT July 26, AT Aug 6–21, possible United training overlap) is the most operationally dense stretch. Front-loading starts now.

On Running the Inventory Project

The inventory system was a good example of what happens when you let a project find its right form. It started as a Notion database, became a flat HTML file, then became a two-file web app backed by Supabase — all in 3.5 weeks. The migration decision was right each time it was made.

528 items cataloged. The AI photo-parse workflow was the forcing function — taking photos and having Claude read them and output structured text cut the data entry time by probably 60%. That workflow is worth using again on any cataloging project.

The column visibility bug that kept recurring was a reminder that bugs at the intersection of state and rendering always hide in the same place — you fix the state but forget to re-run the display logic. Once applyColPrefs() was wired to fire after every renderTable(), it stopped.
